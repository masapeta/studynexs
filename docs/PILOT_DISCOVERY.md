# Pilot Discovery — Questions for the School

> Owner: Avinash Reddy Masapeta (ARM) · For the pilot-school meeting.
> Goal: turn their *wishlist* into a real *buy-list* — the 3 things they'd switch for, and a price.
> Rule: **ask about what they already do and a forced choice — not "what features do you want"** (everyone answers "everything").
> Bring the working demo (Class 10 Maths paper) — **show first, then ask.**

---

## The questions

1. **Today's reality.** Walk me through how your teachers set exam papers and grade them right now — who does it, how long does it take, and which part is genuinely painful?

2. **Where the time goes.** Thinking about last term, where did your teachers' and office staff's time go *outside* actual teaching — grading, paperwork, fee follow-ups, parent messages, attendance, inspection records? What was the single biggest drain?

3. **What you use now.** What software are you running today (ERP or anything else)? What works, what's broken, and what would have to change for you to switch?

4. **The trigger.** What made you start looking at new software *now*, specifically?

5. **Forced rank (the key one).** If I could get only **three** things fully working for you by next term, which three would you pick?

6. **Willingness to pay.** For the AI question-paper + grading help specifically — would you pay around ₹___ per student per month for that? At what price does it become an easy "no"? And who actually approves that spend — you, the management/trust, or is it passed to parents?

7. **Who else.** Who else should I be talking to — your exam in-charge, a couple of teachers, the office admin?

---

## How to read the answers (be honest with yourself)

You're listening for **behavior and commitment**, not compliments:

- **Real interest** = they name a clear trigger, give you a forced top-3, put an actual number on price, hand you another name, or ask "when can I have it." 
- **Polite no** = "sounds great, keep me posted" with no next step, no number, no name. Write it down as a no, not a maybe.
- **Watch Q2:** if exams/grading isn't in their top-2 pains when you *don't* lead them there, the wedge may be wrong — and that's worth more than any feature you'd build.

A forced top-3 plus a real price is your buy-list. The long wishlist is noise.
